# Target List - 2026-12-13-berlin-geminids

Visual-only list (naked eye + 8x42 binoculars, no telescope, per requirements.md).
Primary night is 2026-12-13 (night.md's top-ranked night: 20.2% moon illumination,
moon up only 16% of the dark window). Altitudes are also given for 2026-12-14, the
secondary candidate, in case the sky-forecaster picks it instead — the astro MCP shows
every object's altitude track differs by well under 1 degree between the two nights,
so this plan holds for either. Coordinates used: Zootzen observation site (site-scout's
recommended site), lat 52.786901, lon 12.609480, elevation 30 m, tz_offset +1
(Europe/Berlin standard time). Dark window both nights: ~18:01/18:02-06:06 local
(12.07-12.08 h).

## Targets

### Geminid Meteor Shower (naked eye, primary target)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | meteor shower (radiant in Gemini, near Castor/Pollux) |
| Magnitude | individual meteors typically mag +1 to -2, often bright and colored |
| Max altitude | radiant itself not in the astro MCP catalog; Gemini clears the eastern horizon in early evening and is high overhead by 02:00-03:00 local, per the shower's published behaviour (more meteors visible the higher the radiant) |
| Hours above 20 deg in dark window | effectively the whole dark window from mid-evening onward, since the radiant rises early and climbs through the night |
| Best observing slot | 2026-12-13, 21:00-02:00 local (2026-12-14 peak night per IMO/EarthSky forecasts of 75-150 meteors/hour under dark skies at zenith radiant) |
| Mode notes | Naked eye only. Lie back, look roughly overhead/toward the zenith (not directly at Gemini), let peripheral vision catch trails across the whole sky. No binoculars for the meteors themselves - they move too fast and the field is too narrow. |
| What you will actually see | Under Zootzen's dark sky with the Moon down (moonset 20:01 local on the 13th), expect a real chance at double-digit meteors per hour by late evening, rising toward the shower's 60-100+/hr dark-sky peak rate after midnight as the radiant climbs. |

### The Moon (naked eye, secondary/opportunistic)

Rejected as a formally "observable" target under the 20-degree-for-an-hour rule (see
Rejected Targets), but summarized here since requirements.md asks for it whenever it is
up. On 2026-12-13 it is a thin waxing crescent (20.2% illuminated) at only 12.0 degrees
altitude right as the dark window opens (18:01 local), setting by roughly 20:01. Low in
the southwest, easy naked-eye or a quick look in the 8x42s, but not worth build-around
observing time - see it in the first 10-15 minutes after arrival, then move on to the
meteor watch.

|---|---|

## Observing Order

1. **18:00-18:20 - Arrival and dark adaptation.** No optics yet; let eyes adjust for 20-30 minutes per the plan's baseline, per requirements.md's visual-only mode.
2. **18:20-18:40 - The Moon (naked eye), opportunistic.** Low in the southwest at ~12 degrees, sets by ~20:01; catch it now before it's gone, costs nothing against the meteor watch.
3. **18:30-20:30 - Andromeda Galaxy / M31 (binoculars).** Highest of the deep-sky targets early (78.6 degrees at 19:21), so it goes first before it starts its long slow descent through the rest of the night.
4. **20:00-22:00 - Perseus Double Cluster (binoculars).** Near zenith (85.5 degrees at 21:01) in this slot - easiest overhead view before it's more comfortable to also glance for early Geminids.
5. **20:00-23:00 - Pleiades / M45 (binoculars), interleaved with the above.** Peaks at 61.4 degrees at 22:21; a quick, bright, high-value look that doesn't compete for a long dedicated slot.
6. **21:00 onward - Geminid meteor shower (naked eye), the main event.** Settle in reclined, wide-sky naked-eye watching from here through the rest of the night; this is the primary target and gets the bulk of the session.
7. **22:30-01:30 - Orion Nebula / M42 (binoculars), worked in during meteor watch lulls.** Only clears 20 degrees from ~21:00 and peaks at 31.8 degrees at 00:01 - a brief binocular break during the meteor vigil once it's high enough to be worth the look.
8. **After ~02:00 - Continue naked-eye Geminid watching** as the Gemini radiant climbs highest and meteor rates increase, until cold (summer-jackets-only party, per requirements.md) or schedule ends the session.

## Rejected Targets

- **The Moon** - fails the "clears 20 degrees for at least an hour" rule on both
  candidate nights: max altitude 12.0 degrees on 2026-12-13 (`hours_above_min_altitude`
  = 0) and 19.6 degrees on 2026-12-14 (also 0 hours above 20 degrees), per
  `mcp__astro__object_visibility`. It is real and visible low in the southwest for
  roughly the first hour of each dark window (naked eye or a quick binocular look,
  see slot 2 above) but does not qualify as a built-around target. A December next
  year with a fuller, higher moon during the Geminids (check moon phase calendars
  for December 2027) would give a better dedicated lunar session.
